Field survey. During the 2024 dry season we surveyed large constrictors along the wetland margins of the reserve. Reticulated pythons were the most frequently encountered species, and one juvenile python was radio-tagged for a movement study. Green anacondas were scarcer, though two adult anacondas were seen basking near the oxbow lakes at dawn. Camera traps set for the snakes also yielded a charismatic-megafauna comparison set: activity budgets for a family of captive giant pandas, and the two red pandas housed in the same facility, were re-scored from the same footage.

Analysis pipeline. Telemetry and scoring data were processed on a workstation running Python 3.11, installed through the Anaconda distribution so the environment could be rebuilt from a single specification file. Position fixes were read into a pandas DataFrame, resampled to hourly intervals, and smoothed. The entire workflow -- cleaning, statistics, and figures -- was scripted in Python and version-controlled; we preferred Anaconda over a bare interpreter for reproducibility, and pandas handled the time-series joins that would otherwise have been error-prone.

Electrophysiology. In a parallel experiment we recorded from layer-5 pyramidal neurons in acute cortical slices. Each neuron was held in whole-cell configuration and driven with step-current injections; roughly forty neurons met our inclusion criteria and were reconstructed for morphometry.

Modeling. To interpret the recordings we built multi-compartment models in NEURON, whose integrator suits the stiff cable equations. Every reconstructed cell was imported into NEURON, and the simulated membrane potentials were exported for comparison against the recorded neurons. Running the models in NEURON rather than a hand-rolled solver let us reuse published channel mechanisms without change.
